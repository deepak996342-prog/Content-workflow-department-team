# Gen Z Copy Writing Guide
## Department 07 — Copy Team Reference

---

## The Core Principle

Gen Z has a finely tuned BS detector. The moment your script sounds like a financial advisor, a corporate memo, or a teacher — they're gone. Your copy must sound like a smart friend texting them something they just figured out.

**The standard test:** Would a 22-year-old say this out loud in a voice message to a friend?
- Yes → keep it
- No → rewrite it

## Sentence Structure Rules

### The Rhythm Rule

Vary sentence length intentionally. Long sentences establish context. Short sentences land the point. Really short? Impact.

Example:
> "The average Gen Z worker pays 22% in taxes. But some people pay nothing. Here's how. And it's completely legal."

### The 15-Word Maximum

No single sentence in a reel script should exceed 15 spoken words. If it does — break it in two. Always.

### The One-Idea-Per-Line Rule

Each line of the script carries exactly one idea. When the idea is done, the line is done. No compound sentences in a reel script.

## The Gut-Punch Ending Rule

The last line before the CTA is the climax payoff. It must:
- Be **under 10 words**
- Land like a gut punch
- Be the moment everything clicks for the viewer
- Be impossible to forget

**Examples that work:**

| Topic | Gut-Punch Line |
|-------|---------------|
| Tax hacks | "That's the game. Now you know the rules." |
| AI in finance | "This took 4 minutes. Used to take a week." |
| Corporate law | "They knew this at 25. You know it now." |
| Salary negotiation | "One conversation with HR. That's all it takes." |
| Investing | "The earlier you start, the wilder the result." |
| Financial system | "The system wasn't built for you. Use it anyway." |
| Business structure | "Your boss doesn't pay 30%. Now you don't have to either." |

**The test:** If you could spray-paint this line on a wall, would it make sense and hit hard? If yes — approved.

## CTA Language That Works for Gen Z

### What Works

| CTA Style | Example | Why It Works |
|-----------|---------|-------------|
| FOMO | "Save this before the algorithm buries it." | Creates scarcity urgency |
| Social share | "Send this to someone who's still overpaying." | Triggers sharing with specific purpose |
| Participation | "Comment your salary range — I'll show you your exact saving number." | Personal, interactive |
| Cliffhanger | "Follow — Part 2 drops Friday. It gets darker." | Creates serial engagement |
| Empowerment | "Now go show HR this. You're welcome." | Gives them a next action in the real world |

### What Doesn't Work (Never Use)

| Generic CTA | Why It Fails |
|-------------|-------------|
| "Don't forget to like and subscribe." | Pure algorithm-speak — Gen Z ignores it |
| "Leave a comment below." | Too vague — gives no reason to comment |
| "Hit the follow button for more content like this." | Sounds corporate, gives no value |
| "I'll see you in the next one." | Ends with nothing — no urgency, no action |

## What to Cut Every Single Time

Always cut these from every draft:

1. Any sentence starting with "It's important to note..."
2. "As mentioned earlier" — if it was important, they remembered it
3. "In conclusion" — the script ends, that is the conclusion
4. Parenthetical explanations dropped in the middle of a spoken sentence
5. Qualifiers that soften impact: "kind of", "sort of", "perhaps", "might", "possibly", "somewhat"
6. Double adjectives: "really great" → "great"; "very important" → "important"
7. Filler transitions: "basically", "essentially", "moving on", "so anyway"
8. Anything that sounds like you're setting up context before the interesting thing — cut to the interesting thing

---

## The "You" Rule

Count every noun in the final script. "You" and "your" combined must outcount any other noun in the script. This keeps the content personal and relevant. Every time you catch yourself writing "people", "workers", "employees", or "individuals" — replace it with "you".

---

## Final Copy Checklist

Before handing off to Department 08, run through this checklist. Every item must be ✅:

- [ ] Every sentence passes the "22-year-old texting a friend" test
- [ ] No sentence exceeds 15 words
- [ ] All corporate vocabulary has been swapped using the table above
- [ ] All qualifiers ("kind of", "sort of", etc.) have been removed
- [ ] Verbs are strong throughout — no weak "have", "make", "do" where a power verb fits
- [ ] The gut-punch line before the CTA is under 10 words
- [ ] The CTA creates FOMO, participation, or a cliffhanger
- [ ] "You" appears more than any other noun
- [ ] Dollar figures are used instead of percentages wherever possible
- [ ] The script reads naturally when spoken aloud at conversational pace
- [ ] No line sounds like LinkedIn
