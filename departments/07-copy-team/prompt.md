## Step 1 — Replace the Hook

Take the final hook from Department 06 and replace the Hook section of the Department 05 script. This is your starting script.

---

## Step 2 — Line-by-Line Language Audit

Go through every line. For each line, ask:
- Would a 22-year-old say this in a voice message? If no — rewrite it.
- Is there a simpler, punchier word? If yes — swap it.
- Is this sentence over 15 words? If yes — break it in two.
- Are there any qualifiers ("kind of", "perhaps", "might")? If yes — cut them.

Apply the vocabulary swap table from `genz-copy-guide.md`.

---

## Step 3 — Strengthen the Verbs

Replace weak verbs with strong ones throughout:
- "make money" → "stack cash"
- "lose money" → "burn through it"
- "understand" → "get it"
- "start a business" → "launch something"
- "pay tax" → "hand over" / "fork out"

---

## Step 4 — Check the Rhythm

Read the script aloud. Vary sentence length intentionally:
- Long sentence to set up context
- Short sentence to land the point
- Very short. For impact.

If three consecutive sentences are the same length, vary them.

---

## Step 5 — Gut-Punch Line Check

The last line before the CTA is the climax landing. It must be:
- Under 10 words
- Unforgettable
- The moment everything clicks

Examples of what this should feel like:
- "That's the game. Now you know the rules."
- "They knew this at 25. You know it now."
- "The system wasn't built for you. Use it anyway."

Check the current line. If it doesn't meet this standard, rewrite it.

---

## Step 6 — CTA Refinement

Replace any generic CTA language with something that creates FOMO, participation, or a cliffhanger.

Good CTA language:
- "Save this before the algorithm buries it."
- "Send this to someone who's still overpaying."
- "Comment your salary slab — I'll tell you your exact saving number."
- "Follow — Part 2 drops [day]. It gets worse."

Bad CTA language (never use):
- "Don't forget to like and subscribe."
- "Leave a comment below."
- "Hit the follow button for more content like this."
