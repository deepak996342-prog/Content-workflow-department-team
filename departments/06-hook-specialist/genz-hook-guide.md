# Gen Z Hook Writing Guide
## Department 06 — Hook Specialist Reference

---

## The 1.5-Second Rule

Gen Z decides whether to keep watching within **1.5 seconds** — before you've even finished your first sentence. The hook is not an introduction. It is a pattern interrupt. It is the only thing standing between you and the scroll.

The hook must work on two levels simultaneously:
1. **Audio hook** — the first spoken words must be impossible to ignore
2. **Visual hook** — the first frame must match the energy and stop the eye before the ear even registers

---

## Core Hook Principles

### 1. Start Mid-Thought
Never start with context. Start with the most interesting thing you're about to say.

Bad: "Today I want to talk about something really important regarding taxes."
Good: "The tax system was literally designed to take more from you. Here's proof."

### 2. Create an Information Gap
The hook must make the viewer feel they're missing something they need. Scrolling away must feel like a loss.
- "Nobody talks about this" implies secret knowledge
- Specific numbers imply precision the viewer doesn't have yet
- A half-stated claim demands completion

### 3. The 12-Word Rule
The spoken hook line must be **12 words or fewer**. Every word after 12 is a word the viewer might scroll past. Count them. Cut ruthlessly.

### 4. Never Start With Greetings
These are algorithm killers and Gen Z attention killers:
- "Hey guys"
- "Welcome back"
- "In today's video"
- "Before we start"
- "Don't forget to subscribe"

### 5. Start With "You", a Number, or a Bold Statement
- "You" — makes it instantly personal and relevant
- A number — creates a pattern interrupt (numbers look different from words)
- A bold declarative — states a truth the viewer didn't know they needed

## The 5 Gen Z Hook Formulas

### Formula A — The Accusation Hook
**Structure:** "You're [common behavior] and it's costing you [specific outcome]."

**Why it works:** Personal, accusatory, creates urgency. Viewer feels called out and needs to know the fix.

**Finance examples:**
- "You're leaving ₹40,000 on the table every single year."
- "You're using a savings account and slowly losing money to inflation."
- "You're paying taxes your employer never told you could be avoided."

---

### Formula B — The Secret Hook
**Structure:** "Nobody tells Gen Z [specific truth about money/taxes/business]."

**Why it works:** Positions content as insider knowledge. Triggers "why didn't I know this?" and outrage at the system.

**Finance examples:**
- "Nobody tells Gen Z that corporations pay half the tax rate you do — legally."
- "Nobody tells Gen Z you can pay zero income tax on ₹12 lakh. Here's exactly how."
- "Nobody tells Gen Z that AI can do your entire tax filing in 4 minutes."

---

### Formula C — The Number Shock Hook
**Structure:** "[Specific stat or number] — and most people have no idea."

**Why it works:** Specific numbers create pattern interrupts. "Most people have no idea" creates exclusionary FOMO.

**Finance examples:**
- "93% of salaried employees overpay their taxes by an average of ₹28,000."
- "3 legal strategies. One result: zero tax. Most people know zero of them."
- "₹12 lakh. Zero tax. 5 steps. I'll cover all of them."

---

### Formula D — The Challenge Hook
**Structure:** "If you're under 25 and don't know this, we need to talk."

**Why it works:** Age-specific, creates urgency, implies the viewer is behind, triggers immediate attention.

**Finance examples:**
- "If you're under 27 and haven't done this with your salary yet, stop everything."
- "If you're earning your first paycheck and nobody explained CTC to you, this is urgent."
- "If you're using AI for everything except your finances, you're leaving money behind."

---

### Formula E — The Contrast Hook
**Structure:** "Rich people do [X]. Everyone else does [Y]. Guess which one you are."

**Why it works:** Creates a binary that the viewer immediately places themselves in — then wants to switch sides.

**Finance examples:**
- "Rich people pay 15% tax. Salaried employees pay 30%. Same income. Different rules."
- "Your boss uses an LLP. You use a savings account. That's the whole difference."
- "Billionaires use AI for their finances. You're still using a spreadsheet."

## Hook Scoring Rubric

Score every hook on three criteria before approving it:

| Criterion | 9–10 | 7–8 | 5–6 | Below 5 |
|-----------|------|-----|-----|---------|
| **Curiosity** | Creates a gap the viewer absolutely must close | Strong curiosity, minor ambiguity | Moderate — viewer might scroll | Doesn't create a gap |
| **Pattern Interrupt** | Completely unexpected — stops the scroll | Unexpected but not jarring | Mildly unexpected | Expected, forgettable |
| **Specificity** | Exact number, name, or concrete fact | Fairly specific | Vague but directional | Generic, could be anything |

**Minimum per criterion for approval: 7/10**

---

## The 4-Question Approval Test

Before approving any hook, answer YES or NO to all four questions. All four must be YES:

1. **Scroll test:** If I saw this in my feed with zero context, would I stop scrolling?
2. **Promise test:** Does this hook make a promise the video can keep?
3. **Cut test:** Is there any word that could be cut without losing meaning?
4. **Notification test:** Would this work as a push notification someone would actually click?

If any answer is NO — reject the hook and revise.

## Vocabulary That Activates Gen Z

Use these when they fit naturally. Never force them.

| Word/Phrase | Best Used For |
|-------------|--------------|
| "fr fr" (for real) | Emphasizing a truth — use sparingly |
| "lowkey" | Something understated or surprising |
| "no cap" | Emphasizing a true, bold claim |
| "wild" | Surprising statistics or facts |
| "cooked" | When a system or situation is badly broken |
| "rent free" | Things that stay in your mind |
| "built different" | Outlier strategies or approaches |
| "hitting different" | Something landing in a new, unexpected way |
| "it's giving..." | Describing a vibe or situation |

**Rule:** If you have to think about whether slang fits, it doesn't fit.

---

## Words That Kill Gen Z Hooks

Never use these in a hook:

| Forbidden | Why |
|-----------|-----|
| "Today we're going to..." | Sets up a lecture, not a conversation |
| "In this video..." | Meta-commentary — wastes precious seconds |
| "Let me explain..." | Signals that context is coming — they'll scroll while waiting |
| "It's important to understand..." | Sounds like a professor |
| "Many people often..." | Vague, impersonal, forgettable |
| "As we all know..." | If we all knew it, why are we here? |
| "I want to share..." | Positions the creator, not the viewer |

## Platform-Specific Hook Notes

| Platform | Hook Behavior |
|----------|--------------|
| Instagram Reels | First frame text overlay often appears before audio — make opening visual match hook energy |
| TikTok | Sound-on culture — spoken hook is primary. First word must land. |
| YouTube Shorts | Often viewed with captions — hook must work silent AND spoken |

---

## Hook + Visual Alignment

The spoken hook and the opening visual (Frame 1) must match in energy. A shocked expression should match a shocking number. A conspiratorial tone should match a close-up lean-in. Misaligned hooks and visuals kill retention in the first second.

Coordinate with the Packaging Team (Dept 08) to align the opening frame with the selected hook.
